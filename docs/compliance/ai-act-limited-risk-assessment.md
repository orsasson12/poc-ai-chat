# EU AI Act — Limited Risk Assessment

> **Not legal advice.** The classification and transparency measures in this document are templates. Whether BizAssist qualifies as Limited Risk in your specific deployment — especially if you sell into healthcare, employment, credit decisions, or public services — must be confirmed by a qualified EU AI Act lawyer.

## 1. System overview

**Name**: BizAssist customer support chatbot
**Operator**: Business owner (tenant) deploying BizAssist on their own site
**Provider (AI Act sense)**: BizAssist platform + the underlying model providers (OpenAI, Anthropic)
**Purpose**: Answering end-customer questions using the business owner's knowledge base
**Deployment context**: E-commerce sites, service businesses, SaaS products — general consumer-facing customer support

## 2. Risk classification

Under the EU AI Act, AI systems fall into four categories:

| Category | Applies to BizAssist? | Reasoning |
|----------|----------------------|-----------|
| **Prohibited** (Art. 5) | No | BizAssist does not perform subliminal manipulation, social scoring, or emotion recognition in workplace/education, or any of the other prohibited practices. |
| **High Risk** (Art. 6 + Annex III) | **Not by default**. Becomes High Risk if a tenant uses it for: employment candidate screening, credit scoring, essential services eligibility, law enforcement, migration, access to education, or medical device diagnosis. Tenants in these sectors **must not** use BizAssist under the standard Limited Risk classification — they need a dedicated High Risk configuration. |
| **Limited Risk** (Art. 50 — transparency) | **Yes — default classification.** BizAssist is an AI system that interacts with natural persons. Article 50 transparency obligations apply. |
| **Minimal Risk** | No | It's not "minimal" — end users are told they're talking to AI. |

### Default classification: **Limited Risk**

This triggers the transparency obligations in Article 50 of the AI Act.

## 3. Article 50 transparency obligations

| Obligation | How BizAssist meets it |
|------------|------------------------|
| Users must be informed they are interacting with an AI system | A persistent disclosure banner is rendered at the top of every chat. The banner text is configurable per tenant in the Compliance dashboard but defaults to: *"You're chatting with an AI assistant. Responses are generated, not written by a human."* The banner cannot be hidden when the tenant classification is Limited Risk; the "off" option is disabled by the platform when that classification is set. |
| The information must be provided in a clear and distinguishable manner at the latest at the time of first interaction | The banner is rendered above the first message bubble, before any user input is accepted. |
| Content generated by AI must be marked as artificially generated where applicable | Chat responses are clearly framed as coming from the assistant persona, and the banner applies to every response. |
| Human oversight must be possible | The escalation system (human handoff to a human agent) is available on every conversation. Tenants configure the escalation email / webhook in the assistant settings. See `.claude/rules/03-safety-system.md` for the safety pipeline that triggers escalations on low-confidence or safety events. |

## 4. Data and training

BizAssist does **not** train any model on customer data. The language models used (Claude Sonnet, GPT-4o) are third-party foundation models. Customer messages are sent as prompts for inference only, under the OpenAI and Anthropic business-tier DPAs which prohibit training on submitted content.

The tenant's knowledge base content is embedded into a Pinecone vector store and used only for Retrieval-Augmented Generation within the tenant's namespace. No training.

## 5. Human oversight

Mapping to Article 14 (even though not strictly required for Limited Risk, these satisfy reasonable-practice expectations):

- **Monitoring**: the Security panel surfaces all safety pipeline events to the business owner.
- **Intervention**: the human handoff system lets the business owner's agent take over any conversation.
- **Override**: the business owner can disable the assistant at any time via the dashboard (`isActive: false`), or edit the knowledge base to correct a wrong answer.
- **Stop**: a conversation can be ended by the end-customer at any time.

## 6. Accuracy, robustness and cybersecurity

- **Accuracy**: the five-layer safety pipeline includes a groundedness check (Layer 4) that validates responses against retrieved knowledge chunks and replaces ungrounded responses with the fallback message.
- **Robustness**: multi-provider LLM failover, rate limiting, and prompt injection detection (Layer 1).
- **Cybersecurity**: see `security-measures.md`.

## 7. Records kept for accountability

- Every chat conversation is persisted in the `conversations` and `messages` tables.
- Every safety event is logged to `security_events`.
- Every SAR is logged to `sar_requests`.
- Every deletion is logged to `data_deletion_audit`.

These records are available to the business owner via the dashboard and can be exported via `/api/compliance/tenant-export`.

## 8. What triggers a reclassification

If any of the following become true, BizAssist is no longer Limited Risk for that deployment and must be re-assessed:

- Tenant uses BizAssist to **screen job applicants** → High Risk (Annex III 4).
- Tenant uses BizAssist to **evaluate creditworthiness** → High Risk (Annex III 5(b)).
- Tenant uses BizAssist to **determine access to essential public services** → High Risk (Annex III 5(a)).
- Tenant uses BizAssist in a **medical device** context → High Risk + MDR implications.

**Tenants in these sectors must sign a separate addendum and use a High Risk configuration** that includes fundamental rights impact assessment, human-in-the-loop confirmation for decisions, and conformity assessment. That configuration is out of scope for the default platform.
